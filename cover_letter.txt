Dear Editor,

We submit “The Advantage Spine: Dynamic Coordinate Selection for High-Rate
RLVR” for consideration as a Research Paper in Neurocomputing.

This manuscript studies whether the learning-rate range of reinforcement
learning with verifiable rewards can be enlarged without amplifying every
coordinate of the dense optimizer update. We introduce the Advantage Spine as
the dynamic per-tensor top-40% support of the pre-mask AdamW update. The joint
Spine recipe combines this support with negative-advantage down-weighting. The
central evidence is a
complete 2×2×4 factorial on Qwen3-8B that crosses mask presence,
negative-channel weight, and four learning-rate multipliers, with three matched
seeds in every cell.

Dense training decreases from 0.426 to 0.340 Mean4 between 1× and 20×, whereas
the joint recipe increases from 0.426 to 0.445. Its paired gain over dense 1×
is 0.019, with a nominal 95% paired interval of [0.0124, 0.0256]. An
exploratory lightly tuned dense 20× arm with 16 warmup steps, gradient clipping at 0.5,
and beta1=0.8 recovers to 0.383 but remains below both dense 1× and the Spine
recipe. The selected 39.3% of coordinates retain 85.0% of update energy.
Matched Spine–dense-emergent support reaches 0.600±0.010 Jaccard, compared
with 0.420±0.008 for mismatched seeds and 0.419±0.005 for dense–dense pairs.
The per-seed specificity gain is 0.180±0.012. Mask controls and transfers to
DAPO, Qwen3-1.7B, and Llama3.1-8B provide
additional evidence for a non-arbitrary, protocol-dependent high-rate
operating region.

The work fits Neurocomputing through its focus on neural optimization,
learning dynamics, dynamic sparse updates, and large-model training. The
manuscript distinguishes step-wise optimizer-update selection from permanent
weight pruning. It reports the full factorial interaction, treats 20× as the
largest tested rate rather than a universal stability boundary, and does not
claim superiority over every retuned dense optimizer.

The public repository at
https://github.com/vivia-an/advantage_spine_v1 contains the method
implementation, manuscript, result tables, protocol metadata, and verification
scripts.
