Dear Editor,

We submit “The Advantage Spine: Dynamic Coordinate Selection for High-Rate
RLVR” for consideration as a Research Paper in Neurocomputing.

The paper studies optimization dynamics in reinforcement learning with
verifiable rewards. Its main evidence is a 2×2×4 factorial on
Qwen3-8B: dense versus dynamic top-40% updates, negative-advantage weight 1.0
versus 0.1, and four learning-rate multipliers. Every one of the 16 cells has
three matched seeds, with agreement among requested, optimizer, scheduler, and
logged learning rates. Evaluation uses a fixed four-benchmark protocol.

Dense training falls from 0.426 to 0.340 Mean4 between 1× and 20×. The joint
Advantage Spine recipe rises from 0.426 to 0.445. Its paired gain over dense 1×
is 0.019 with a nominal 95% paired t interval of [0.0124, 0.0256] for the
endpoint contrast after the full ladder. Neither intervention alone recovers
the dense 1× reference at 20×. The selected 39.3% of coordinates retain
85.0% of update energy. Matched Spine–dense-emergent Jaccard is 0.600±0.010,
compared with 0.420±0.008 for mismatched seeds and 0.419±0.005 for dense–dense
reproducibility; the per-seed specificity gain is 0.180±0.012. Mask controls
and transfer to DAPO, Qwen3-1.7B, and Llama3.1-8B support a non-arbitrary,
protocol-dependent effect.

The accompanying open repository
(https://github.com/vivia-an/advantage_spine_v1) contains the method
implementation, seed-level results, resolved configurations, checkpoint and
mask identities, and scripts that recompute every reported aggregate.
The manuscript reports the negative factorial interaction and treats 20× as
the largest tested rate rather than a universal boundary.

We also address the apparent tension in the negative-gradient literature.
Rather than claiming that negative samples are generally harmful, the complete
LR ladder shows a step-size-conditional effect: global attenuation changes
Mean4 by only +0.002 at 1× but protects by +0.060 at 20×. This reconciles useful
negative-sample reinforcement with the risk of amplifying a conflict-prone
high-rate coordinate tail.

The work fits Neurocomputing through its focus on neural optimization, dynamic
sparse updates, reproducible learning dynamics, and large-model training. The
manuscript distinguishes optimizer-update sparsity from permanent weight
pruning and connects the result to learning-rate stability and dynamic sparse
training.

We state the experimental boundary directly. All primary runs end after 160
RL optimizer steps on mathematical reasoning tasks. A lightly tuned dense 20×
arm (warmup / gradient clipping / β1) recovers Mean4 from 0.340 to 0.383 but
still trails dense 1× (0.426) and the Spine recipe at 20× (0.445; gap 0.062).
Accordingly, the paper claims a matched-protocol high-rate operating region,
not universal superiority over every retuned dense optimizer. The artifact also
reports hardware, compute scope, step-time and memory overhead, and upstream
model, data, and software terms.
